Distributions

The distributions of Hakaru10 Distribution.hs are values of two type classes: a Distribution has a log-density and a sampler, and a resampleable one (an RDistribution) has a proposal kernel too, by default an independence proposal that samples again. The existential SomeRDistr hides the instance (Distribution.hs:14-41):

class Distribution d a | d -> a where
  logDensity :: d -> a -> LogLikelihood
  sample     :: RandomGen g => d -> g -> (a, LogLikelihood, g)

-- re-sampleable distribution. This is the place to specify
-- custom proposals
class Distribution d a => RDistribution d a | d -> a where
  resample :: RandomGen g =>
     d ->
     a -> LogLikelihood -> -- old value and its LL
     g ->
     ((a,              -- new sample
       LogLikelihood,  -- its likelihood
       g),             -- new seed
      LogLikelihood)   -- (fwd - bwd) for the proposal step.
  -- By default, resample is really sample again
  resample d _ llold g =
    let s@(_,ll,_) = sample d g in
    (s,ll-llold)

In Scheme SomeRDistr is the record rdistr of closures (log-density, sample, resample, plus a finite support for the exact enumerator), and the generator is an rng source that the sampler draws from in place. The engine wraps a distribution in a distk (Metropolis.hs:46-60), whose kind is resampleable, observed (a conditioned distribution: its value is fixed, its log-likelihood follows its parameters) or dirac (a deterministic node). The user-level distributions are the smart constructors bern, normal and so on, upstream's bernS, normalS, ... (Metropolis.hs:70-95), which (aux hakaru10) re-exports as named.

Log-densities are the true ones, normalization included, as upstream insists (Distribution.hs:93-96):

-- Make sure we report the real LL rather than up to the constant
-- (which may depend on the argument to |categorical|).
-- When logDensity is called during the scoring of a proposal,
-- the parameters of the distribution have likely changed

because the parameters of a node's distribution change when its parents are resampled, and the Metropolis-Hastings ratio compares log-likelihoods computed under different parameters.

The samplers are the classical ones: Marsaglia's polar method for the normal distribution (Marsaglia and Bray, 1964); G. Marsaglia and W. W. Tsang, A Simple Method for Generating Gamma Variables, ACM TOMS 26(3), 2000 for the gamma distribution; Joehnk's method (1964) for a beta distribution with both parameters at most 1, and the ratio of two gamma variates otherwise; Knuth's multiplication method (The Art of Computer Programming, vol. 2, §3.4.1) for the Poisson distribution. The log-gamma function is Macleod's Algorithm AS 245 (Applied Statistics 38(2), 1989), transcribed rather than taken from libm, accurate to 10-12 significant digits.

Every Double expression is transcribed in Haskell's association, and every sampler draws exactly where upstream draws: with the random-1.0.1.1 StdGen of (aux hakaru10 random), the samples are upstream's, bit for bit (D16). Four upstream defects are fixed, each tagged in the source with its deviation number: the gamma density (deviation 2), the gamma sampler for a shape below 1 (deviation 3), the log-likelihood of a Poisson sample (deviation 4) and the categorical walk exhausted by rounding (deviation 5).
