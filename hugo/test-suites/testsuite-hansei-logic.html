Logic programming

After HANSEI as a Declarative Logic Programming Language, the logic programs of the page, of its files and of the parser combinators `Reversible' parser combinators.

Re-thinking Prolog

After Re-thinking Prolog (LG l.33-75) and its paper (RT):

❝
One lesson is that guessing-- non-determinism-- is fundamental, but should not be the default mode of execution. One should guess, but guess lazily.

Oleg Kiselyov, Yukiyoshi Kameyama

The library interface the paper relies on (RT Figure 1, l.285-308) is dist, fail, reify0, letlazy, flip, uniformly, exact_reify and reify_part, here probcc-distribution, probcc-impossible, probcc-reify/0, probcc-letlazy (and probcc-letlazy/nesting), probcc-coin, probcc-uniform/either, probcc-reify/exact and probcc-reify/part. The cheap non-determinism of RT §3.1, a generator that guesses whole lists before testing them, finds the splits of a list only within a bound, and diverges past it.

No logic variables? No unification?

After No logic variables? No unification? (LG l.230-327): a logic variable is a generator of its domain, the sharing of the generator expresses its repeated occurrences, and lazy evaluation delays the generation as unification would:

❝
It appears that lazy evaluation can make up for logic variables, rendering Herbrand's original approach practical.

Oleg Kiselyov

The append relation

After The append relation (LG l.328-624) and logic_vars.ml: the Prolog relation 

"append([],L,L).\nappend([H|T],L,[H|R]) :- append(T,L,R)."
becomes a function over lists with a non-deterministic spine, which runs forwards, backwards and sideways once its arguments are logic variables. Prolog's committed choice, needed in practice, breaks running backwards (PD:187-217): 
"del([H|T],H,T).\ndel([H|T],D,[H|R]) :- del(T,D,R).\nprog3(L,S) :- del(L,true,LRem) -> S=deleted(LRem); S=failed.\n%% ?- prog3(L,S).\n%% L = [true|_G16],\n%% S = deleted(_G16)."
where HANSEI's soft-cut, a nested inference, keeps every answer.

Solving scheduling problems

After Solving scheduling problems (LG l.625-757): state the constraints as evidence and ask for the worlds consistent with them; a world is rejected as soon as one fact contradicts the evidence, so only a part of the 8957952 worlds is generated.

Zebra puzzle

After Zebra puzzle (LG l.758-792): the all-different constraint of the five attributes is a lazy permutation of (aux hansei lazy), and the puzzle a naive generate-and-test; its unique solution, at weight 1/199065600000, is in the long suite hansei-long.scm. The U2 bridge puzzle (puzzle.ml) and type inference run backwards (type_inference.ml) are two more programs that guess.

Reversible parser combinators

After `Reversible' parser combinators (LG l.793-996) and parsec.ml, the parser combinators of (aux hansei parsec) work over a non-deterministic stream:

❝
They can emulate not only parsing of a stream (that is, parsing forwards) but also determining a stream for the given parse (parsing backwards).

Oleg Kiselyov

A stream is a thunk answering '() or a pair (char . stream), and a parser maps a stream to the pair (v . stream) of its value and the rest. Alternation is a uniform choice, so a parse comes with the probability of the choices that led to it. parsec-run/backward generates streams with parsec-stream-over, lazily, each cell a probcc-letlazy/nesting, and keeps those the parser accepts whole.

Maximal munch, committed choice and nested inference

After Maximal munch, committed choice and nested inference (LG l.997-1230): parsec-many is Prolog's 

"many(P,S,Rest) :- call(P,S,Srem) *-> many(P,Srem,Rest) ; S = Rest."
with the soft-cut probcc-soft-cut, a nested inference: probcc-first-success over the reified parser. Unlike Prolog's, it keeps parsing backwards complete, since an inner reification lets the stream's choices take effect in the outer world:
❝
many p (s1 | s2) = many p s1 | many p s2

Oleg Kiselyov
