Semantics of branches

In upstream's kernel an inactive node's updates are delayed (M:298-303) and replayed when its branch is activated again (M:767-775). For an observed node this is exact: activation adds back the LL that deactivation took. For a resampleable node the replayed update adds ll(now) − ll(stale) to the Δ of the switching step: it charges the change of LL of steps already accepted without it. The note M:1145-1168 justifies the treatment of branches by merging a branch's nodes into one node:

So, when it comes to computing the acceptance ratio for a program
with branches, we take

p4 = do
     c <- bernN (Val 0.5)
     if_ c (do e1 e2 ...)
           (do e3 e4 e5 ...)

to be

p4' = do
      c <- bernN (Val 0.5)
      if_ c e_then e_else

That is, we merge (at least for the purpose of presentation) e1, e2 ...
into one node e_then, whose ll is the sum of ll of constituents. Ditto
for e_else.

When we switch the branches, we remove the ll of one branch from
llcum and add ll of the other branch. In our terms, we remove the ll
of the deactivated nodes and add ll of the activated nodes.

This algorithm is resilient with respect to adding replacing a node e
with e >>= diracN -- the desired behavior.

The argument assumes the reactivated nodes are fresh draws from their prior, while the kernel reuses their stale values. The smallest counterexample, pswitch (plan §6.8), has P(c) = 0.5; the exact stationary distribution of upstream's kernel gives P(c) = 0.377286. The upstream examples are unaffected: their branch-local resampleable nodes have constant parameters.

(define pswitch
  (do/monad
    (← c (dist bern 0.5))
    (← m (dist uniformly '(0 1)))
    (if_ c
         (dist categorical (lift (λ (mv) `((,mv . 0.9) (,(- 1 mv) . 0.1))) m))
         (diracN (lift identity m)))
    (return c)))

B4′, the negative controls of B4: each oracle of #:check reports a chain it should reject, with an internal condition. construction-check (plan §6.9, §15 C37) catches a model whose two builds, the initial trace and a rebuild from the same seed, differ in value (a counter answered as the result) or in LL (the same counter as an observation's standard deviation): each run increments the counter after a bind, so each build sees another value. An LL -inf agrees only with -inf: in counter/bound the counter bounds the uniform of an observation in a branch never taken, which is impossible on the first build only, and 'extended mode, which keeps llcum finite, reports it. mcmC runs it once per chain under #:check, and reports both models the same way. A deferred value (deviation 11) is compared by its arguments, since its closure is fresh per build: counter/deferred reads the counter in the inactive branch with a function that raises, and its two builds differ only there. The rebuild also catches a model whose graph alone changes, switching, whose Dirac node reads x on odd runs and y, of the same value, on even ones; and the address-order redraw catches a sampler that draws once more on its third call, the redraw's (third-skips, another value), or that then reports its LL plus 1 (third-shifts, another LL). In 'extended mode the LL that the redraw checks is the extended one, which never counts an inactive observation, so neither does the check's tolerance: the shift is caught next to an inactive observation that is impossible (LL -inf) or far off (LL about -5·10²³). In 'upstream mode, whose LL adds and then subtracts that observation's (M:659), the shift is lost: next to the impossible observation the LL is NaN, which the redraw cannot compare, and next to the far one it is lost in the rounding. A build of LL NaN is still checked, all but the redraw's LL, since initial-step keeps it (it retries only an infinite LL, M:853-854): next to the impossible observation, the redraw of third-skips reports its value.

reference-check catches each of nine corruptions of the initial trace of shifted-normal, x and the Dirac node x + 1, made behind the engine's back with its exported setters, each with its own message: an LL shifted by 1 or made -inf, a value, a distribution or a tstamp after now in a node's state, a deactivation count, a delayed update on an active node, a pending weight left over, and the chain's card change. It also catches, through the wiring of mcmC, a user distribution whose density drifts with the number of its calls. In 'extended mode, where no update is delayed, it checks the inactive nodes' states too: the LL of the inactive branch's choice of tac1, shifted by 1, is reported in an 'extended build and not in an 'upstream one, where an inactive node may hold a stale state.

The step check catches a rejected step that did not restore the state: in behind-the-journal the sampler of the proposed node replaces the state of the observed x by an equal copy with the raw node-state-set!, which no journal entry undoes; the reference evaluator compares values and LLs and accepts the copy, the step check compares the state objects themselves (C5) and does not, in both semantics. Without #:check the chain runs.

In 'extended mode it also catches an accepted step whose Δ is not the change of the extended LL: in tampered the sampler of the only resampleable node, armed before step k, adds 1 to the stored LL of the observed x, which the step's Δ does not count; with no observation of the proposed node every step is accepted. The step check runs before the reference evaluator, which would report the LL instead. #:check 5 checks the first 5 steps only: it reports a corruption at step 5 and runs through one at step 6.
