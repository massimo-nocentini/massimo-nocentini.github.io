Replaying upstream's records

HANSEI's samplers draw from OCaml's Random module, through random_selector seed, which re-initialises its global state (P:221-222). (aux hansei random) transcribes that generator, the lagged Fibonacci generator of 55 words seeded with MD5 RFC 1321, with the xor bits and the three-draw Random.float of the OCaml releases HANSEI's records were made with (OCaml 4.14 draws two words), the only variant that replays the records of samplingM.ml and nested.ml bit for bit; see the plan, D6 and D20, and Embedded probabilistic domain-specific language HANSEI.

Why three draws: OCaml 4.14's Random.float (OR:167-172) draws two 30-bit words, (r1 /. 2^30 +. r2) /. 2^30, and with it the rejection sampling of dcoin_and 10 over 10000 samples, seed 17, gives 0.054 where SM records 0.0514; three words, ((r0 /. 2^30 +. r1) /. 2^30 +. r2) /. 2^30, replay every assertion of samplingM.ml, nested.ml, slazy.ml, parsec.ml and music1a.ml under an OCaml build of HANSEI patched so (deviation 32). The global state is upstream's too: a selector made with a seed re-initialises the one state every selector draws from, which N:30-31 needs (D6).

The expected values come from three sources: upstream's own assertions; runs of upstream HANSEI under OCaml 4.14.2 with delimcc, two-draw and three-draw; and a Python transcription of the samplers over OCaml's generator, written while planning the port and kept in test/hansei-python/ (its README says how to run it under python3 -I; it is not part of make test).
