The first four chapters build the conceptual and computational bedrock of Bayesian statistics: why probability is the calculus of rational belief, how exchangeability justifies conditionally i.i.d. models via de Finetti’s theorem, how conjugate priors yield closed-form posteriors and predictive distributions in one-parameter exponential families, and how Monte Carlo sampling turns arbitrary posterior expectations and model checks into simple averages.
